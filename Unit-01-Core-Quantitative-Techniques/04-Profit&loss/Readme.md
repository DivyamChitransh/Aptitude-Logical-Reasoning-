# Profit and Loss

## Introduction

Profit and Loss is an important topic in Quantitative Aptitude. It is mainly used to understand the relationship between the **Cost Price** and **Selling Price** of an item.

Whenever we buy and sell something, there can be three possible situations:

* The item is sold for more than its cost price → **Profit**
* The item is sold for less than its cost price → **Loss**
* The item is sold at the same price at which it was bought → **No Profit, No Loss**

Profit and Loss questions are commonly used in aptitude exams and real-life situations involving:

* Business
* Shopping
* Discounts
* Pricing
* Sales
* Marked Price
* Percentage calculations

---

# 1. Cost Price (CP)

The price at which an item is purchased is called its **Cost Price**.

It is represented as:

```text
CP
```

### Example

If a shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹500:

```text
Cost Price = ₹500
```

---

# 2. Selling Price (SP)

The price at which an item is sold is called its **Selling Price**.

It is represented as:

```text
SP
```

### Example

If the shopkeeper sells the bag for ₹650:

```text
Selling Price = ₹650
```

---

# 3. What is Profit?

When the Selling Price is greater than the Cost Price:

```text
SP > CP
```

Then there is a **Profit**.

Formula:

```text
Profit = SP - CP
```

### Example

A person buys an item for ₹400 and sells it for ₹500.

```text
Profit = SP - CP

= 500 - 400

= ₹100
```

Therefore, the profit is **₹100**.

---

# 4. Profit Percentage

Profit percentage is calculated on the **Cost Price**.

Formula:

```text
Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100
```

Since:

```text
Profit = SP - CP
```

We can also write:

```text
Profit % = ((SP - CP) / CP) × 100
```

### Example

An item is bought for ₹400 and sold for ₹500.

```text
Profit = 500 - 400

= ₹100
```

Now:

```text
Profit % = (100 / 400) × 100

= 25%
```

Therefore, the profit percentage is **25%**.

---

# 5. What is Loss?

When the Selling Price is less than the Cost Price:

```text
SP < CP
```

Then there is a **Loss**.

Formula:

```text
Loss = CP - SP
```

### Example

An item is bought for ₹600 and sold for ₹450.

```text
Loss = CP - SP

= 600 - 450

= ₹150
```

Therefore, the loss is **₹150**.

---

# 6. Loss Percentage

Loss percentage is also calculated on the **Cost Price**.

Formula:

```text
Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100
```

Or:

```text
Loss % = ((CP - SP) / CP) × 100
```

### Example

An item is bought for ₹800 and sold for ₹600.

```text
Loss = 800 - 600

= ₹200
```

Therefore:

```text
Loss % = (200 / 800) × 100

= 25%
```

---

# 7. Finding Selling Price

## When Profit Percentage is Given

```text
SP = CP × (100 + Profit %) / 100
```

### Example

An item is purchased for ₹500 and sold at a profit of 20%.

```text
SP = 500 × 120 / 100

= ₹600
```

---

## When Loss Percentage is Given

```text
SP = CP × (100 - Loss %) / 100
```

### Example

An item is purchased for ₹500 and sold at a loss of 10%.

```text
SP = 500 × 90 / 100

= ₹450
```

---

# 8. Finding Cost Price

## When Profit Percentage is Given

```text
CP = SP × 100 / (100 + Profit %)
```

### Example

An item is sold for ₹600 at a profit of 20%.

```text
CP = 600 × 100 / 120

= ₹500
```

---

## When Loss Percentage is Given

```text
CP = SP × 100 / (100 - Loss %)
```

### Example

An item is sold for ₹720 at a loss of 10%.

```text
CP = 720 × 100 / 90

= ₹800
```

---

# 9. No Profit and No Loss

When:

```text
CP = SP
```

Then:

```text
Profit = 0

Loss = 0
```

### Example

An item is bought for ₹1,000 and sold for ₹1,000.

Therefore:

```text
No Profit, No Loss
```

---

# 10. Marked Price (MP)

The price written or displayed on an item is called the **Marked Price**.

It is represented as:

```text
MP
```

A shopkeeper may offer a discount on the marked price.

Usually:

```text
MP ≥ SP
```

---

# 11. Discount

A discount is a reduction in the marked price of an item.

Formula:

```text
Discount = MP - SP
```

Discount percentage:

```text
Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100
```

### Example

The marked price of an item is ₹1,000.

A discount of 20% is given.

```text
Discount = 20% of 1000

= ₹200
```

Therefore:

```text
SP = MP - Discount

= 1000 - 200

= ₹800
```

---

# 12. Finding Selling Price After Discount

Formula:

```text
SP = MP × (100 - Discount %) / 100
```

### Example

The marked price of an item is ₹2,000 and a discount of 15% is given.

```text
SP = 2000 × 85 / 100

= ₹1,700
```

---

# 13. Successive Discounts

Sometimes two or more discounts are given one after another.

For two successive discounts:

```text
a% and b%
```

Equivalent Discount:

```text
Equivalent Discount = a + b - (a × b / 100)
```

### Example

An item is given successive discounts of:

```text
20% and 10%
```

Equivalent discount:

```text
= 20 + 10 - (20 × 10 / 100)

= 30 - 2

= 28%
```

Therefore, successive discounts of **20% and 10%** are equal to a single discount of **28%**.

---

# 14. Discount and Profit

A discount does not always mean that the seller is making a loss.

The shopkeeper may mark the price higher than the cost price and then offer a discount.

### Example

```text
CP = ₹800

MP = ₹1,200

Discount = 20%
```

First find the selling price:

```text
SP = 1200 × 80 / 100

= ₹960
```

Now:

```text
Profit = SP - CP

= 960 - 800

= ₹160
```

Profit percentage:

```text
Profit % = (160 / 800) × 100

= 20%
```

Therefore, even after giving a **20% discount**, the seller still makes a **20% profit**.

---

# 15. Profit and Loss When Percentage is Given

A useful shortcut is to assume:

```text
CP = 100
```

Then calculations become easier.

### Example

If there is a profit of 25%:

```text
CP = 100

SP = 125
```

If there is a loss of 20%:

```text
CP = 100

SP = 80
```

This method is especially useful in ratio and percentage-based questions.

---

# 16. Important Formulas

## Profit

```text
Profit = SP - CP
```

## Loss

```text
Loss = CP - SP
```

## Profit Percentage

```text
Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100
```

## Loss Percentage

```text
Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100
```

## Selling Price with Profit

```text
SP = CP × (100 + Profit %) / 100
```

## Selling Price with Loss

```text
SP = CP × (100 - Loss %) / 100
```

## Cost Price with Profit

```text
CP = SP × 100 / (100 + Profit %)
```

## Cost Price with Loss

```text
CP = SP × 100 / (100 - Loss %)
```

## Discount

```text
Discount = MP - SP
```

## Discount Percentage

```text
Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100
```

## Selling Price After Discount

```text
SP = MP × (100 - Discount %) / 100
```

## Successive Discount

```text
Equivalent Discount =
a + b - (a × b / 100)
```

---

# 17. Important Shortcuts and Tips

### Tip 1: Profit and Loss Percentage Are Always Calculated on CP

Remember:

```text
Profit % = Profit / CP × 100

Loss % = Loss / CP × 100
```

Do not calculate profit or loss percentage using the selling price unless the question specifically asks for it.

---

### Tip 2: Assume CP = 100

For percentage-based questions:

```text
CP = 100
```

This makes calculations easier.

For example:

```text
Profit = 20%

SP = 120
```

---

### Tip 3: Discount is Calculated on MP

```text
Discount % = Discount / MP × 100
```

Remember:

```text
Profit/Loss → Calculated on CP

Discount → Calculated on MP
```

This is a very important distinction.

---

### Tip 4: Successive Discounts Cannot Be Directly Added

For example:

```text
20% + 10% ≠ 30%
```

The actual equivalent discount is:

```text
20 + 10 - (20 × 10 / 100)

= 28%
```

---

# 18. Common Mistakes

* Calculating profit percentage using SP instead of CP.
* Calculating discount percentage using CP instead of MP.
* Adding successive discounts directly.
* Confusing Cost Price, Selling Price and Marked Price.
* Assuming that giving a discount always results in a loss.
* Forgetting to convert percentages correctly while calculating SP or CP.

---

# 19. Summary

* **Cost Price (CP)** is the price at which an item is purchased.
* **Selling Price (SP)** is the price at which an item is sold.
* If `SP > CP`, there is a **Profit**.
* If `SP < CP`, there is a **Loss**.
* Profit and Loss percentages are calculated on the **Cost Price**.
* Discount is calculated on the **Marked Price**.
* Successive discounts require a special formula.
* An item can still generate a profit even after a discount is given.
