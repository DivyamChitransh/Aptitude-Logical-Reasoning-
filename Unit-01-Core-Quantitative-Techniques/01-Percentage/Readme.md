# Percentage

## Introduction

Percentage is a way of expressing a quantity as a part of 100.

The word **percent** means **"per hundred"** and is represented using the symbol `%`.

For example:

* 10% means 10 out of 100
* 25% means 25 out of 100
* 50% means 50 out of 100
* 100% represents the complete quantity

Mathematically:

```text
25% = 25/100 = 0.25
```

Percentage helps us compare different quantities using a common base of 100.

---

## Why Do We Use Percentage?

Consider the following example:

* Student A scored **80 marks out of 100**
* Student B scored **160 marks out of 200**

We cannot directly compare 80 and 160 because the total marks are different.

To compare their performance, we convert both scores into percentages.

### Student A

```text
Percentage = (80 / 100) × 100
           = 80%
```

### Student B

```text
Percentage = (160 / 200) × 100
           = 80%
```

Therefore, both students have the same performance when compared using percentages.

Percentage is commonly used in:

* Examination results
* Discounts and sales
* Profit and Loss
* Salary increments
* Population growth
* Interest rates
* Data analysis
* Business reports

# 1. Basic Concept of Percentage

The basic meaning of percentage is:

> Percentage = Per Hundred

The symbol `%` represents division by 100.

Therefore:

```text
x% = x/100
```

For example:

```text
10% = 10/100 = 1/10

25% = 25/100 = 1/4

50% = 50/100 = 1/2

75% = 75/100 = 3/4

100% = 100/100 = 1
```

---

# 2. Conversion Between Percentage, Fraction and Decimal

A percentage can be represented in three different forms:

```text
Percentage → Fraction → Decimal
```

Understanding these conversions helps in solving aptitude questions more quickly.

## Percentage to Fraction

To convert a percentage into a fraction:

```text
x% = x/100
```

Then simplify the fraction.

### Example

Convert 25% into a fraction.

```text
25%

= 25/100

= 1/4
```

**Answer: 1/4**

---

## Percentage to Decimal

To convert a percentage into a decimal, divide it by 100.

### Example

Convert 75% into a decimal.

```text
75%

= 75/100

= 0.75
```

**Answer: 0.75**

---

## Decimal to Percentage

To convert a decimal into a percentage, multiply it by 100.

### Example

Convert 0.45 into a percentage.

```text
0.45 × 100

= 45%
```

**Answer: 45%**

---

## Fraction to Percentage

To convert a fraction into a percentage:

```text
Percentage = Fraction × 100
```

### Example

Convert 3/4 into a percentage.

```text
3/4 × 100

= 75%
```

**Answer: 75%**

# 4. Finding the Percentage of a Number

To find **x% of a number**:

```text
x% of y = (x/100) × y
```

## Example 1

Find 25% of 200.

```text
25% of 200

= (25/100) × 200

= 50
```

**Answer: 50**

---

## Example 2

Find 15% of 400.

```text
15% of 400

= (15/100) × 400

= 60
```

**Answer: 60**

---

## Quick Calculation Method

Some percentages can be calculated directly.

### 10% of a Number

Divide the number by 10.

```text
10% of 500

= 500/10

= 50
```

### 1% of a Number

Divide the number by 100.

```text
1% of 500

= 500/100

= 5
```

### 50% of a Number

Find half of the number.

```text
50% of 200

= 100
```

### 25% of a Number

Find one-fourth of the number.

```text
25% of 200

= 200/4

= 50
```

# 5. Finding What Percentage One Quantity Is of Another

When a part and the total quantity are given:

```text
Percentage = (Part / Total) × 100
```

## Example

A student scored 72 marks out of 90.

Find the percentage.

```text
Percentage = (72/90) × 100

           = 80%
```

**Answer: 80%**

---

# 6. Percentage Increase

Percentage increase tells us how much a quantity has increased compared to its original value.

```text
Percentage Increase = (Increase / Original Value) × 100
```

Where:

```text
Increase = New Value - Original Value
```

## Example

The price of a product increases from ₹200 to ₹250.

Find the percentage increase.

```text
Increase = 250 - 200

         = 50
```

Now:

```text
Percentage Increase = (50/200) × 100

                    = 25%
```

**Answer: 25% Increase**

---

# 7. Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease tells us how much a quantity has decreased compared to its original value.

```text
Percentage Decrease = (Decrease / Original Value) × 100
```

Where:

```text
Decrease = Original Value - New Value
```

## Example

The price of a product decreases from ₹500 to ₹400.

Find the percentage decrease.

```text
Decrease = 500 - 400

         = 100
```

Now:

```text
Percentage Decrease = (100/500) × 100

                    = 20%
```

**Answer: 20% Decrease**

---

# 8. Successive Percentage Change

Sometimes a quantity is increased or decreased more than once.

For example:

```text
First increased by 10%

Then increased by 20%
```

The overall percentage change is not always obtained by simply adding the percentages.

The formula for successive percentage changes is:

```text
Net Percentage Change = a + b + (a × b)/100
```

Where:

* `a` = First percentage change
* `b` = Second percentage change

An increase is considered positive and a decrease is considered negative.

## Example 1: Successive Increase

A price is increased by 10% and then increased by 20%.

```text
Net Change = 10 + 20 + (10 × 20)/100

           = 30 + 2

           = 32%
```

**Answer: 32% Increase**

---

## Example 2: Increase Followed by Decrease

A price is increased by 20% and then decreased by 10%.

```text
Net Change = 20 + (-10) + (20 × -10)/100

           = 10 - 2

           = 8%
```

**Answer: 8% Increase**

# Important Points to Remember

1. Percentage means **per hundred**.

2. The symbol `%` means division by 100.

```text
x% = x/100
```

3. To find **x% of a number**:

```text
x% of y = (x/100) × y
```

4. To find what percentage one quantity is of another:

```text
Percentage = (Part / Total) × 100
```

5. For percentage increase:

```text
Percentage Increase = (Increase / Original Value) × 100
```

6. For percentage decrease:

```text
Percentage Decrease = (Decrease / Original Value) × 100
```

7. Always compare the increase or decrease with the **original value**.

8. Successive percentage changes should not always be added directly.
