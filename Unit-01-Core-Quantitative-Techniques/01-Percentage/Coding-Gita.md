Percentages

## 1. What is a Percentage

"Percent" means "out of 100." When we say 20%, we mean 20 out of every 100.

So, x% simply means the fraction x/100.

For example:
- 50% means 50/100, which is the same as 1/2
- 25% means 25/100, which is the same as 1/4
- 10% means 10/100, which is the same as 1/10

## 2. Converting Between Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

**Fraction to Percentage:** Multiply the fraction by 100.

Example: Convert 3/4 to percentage.
3/4 × 100 = 75%

**Percentage to Fraction:** Divide the percentage by 100 and simplify.

Example: Convert 40% to a fraction.
40/100 = 2/5

**Decimal to Percentage:** Multiply the decimal by 100.

Example: Convert 0.65 to percentage.
0.65 × 100 = 65%

**Percentage to Decimal:** Divide the percentage by 100.

Example: Convert 12% to decimal.
12/100 = 0.12

## 4. How to Find a Percentage of a Number

To find x% of a number N, use this formula:

x% of N = (x / 100) × N

**Example 1**
Find 35% of 240.

Step 1: Write 35% as a fraction: 35/100
Step 2: Multiply by 240
35/100 × 240 = 84

So, 35% of 240 is 84.

**Example 2**
A number, when increased by 20%, becomes 180. Find the original number.

Let the original number be N.

If we increase N by 20%, we add 20% of N to N.
So the new number = N + 20% of N = N + 0.20N = 1.20N

We are told this equals 180.
1.20N = 180
N = 180 / 1.20
N = 150

So the original number is 150.

## 5. Percentage Increase and Percentage Decrease

**When a value increases by x%:**

New value = Original value + x% of Original value
New value = Original value × (1 + x/100)

**When a value decreases by x%:**

New value = Original value − x% of Original value
New value = Original value × (1 − x/100)

**Example 3**
A shopkeeper increases the price of an item from Rs. 250 to Rs. 300. Find the percentage increase.

Step 1: Find how much the price increased.
Increase = 300 − 250 = 50

Step 2: Divide this increase by the original price, then multiply by 100.
Percentage increase = (50 / 250) × 100 = 20%

**Example 4**
The population of a town decreases from 50,000 to 46,000. Find the percentage decrease.

Step 1: Find the decrease.
Decrease = 50000 − 46000 = 4000

Step 2: Divide by the original value and multiply by 100.
Percentage decrease = (4000 / 50000) × 100 = 8%

**General Formula for Percentage Change**

Percentage change = [(New value − Original value) / Original value] × 100

If the answer is positive, it is a percentage increase.
If the answer is negative, it is a percentage decrease.

## 6. When a Value Changes Twice (Successive Percentage Change)

Sometimes a quantity changes two times in a row. For example, a price is increased by 10%, and then decreased by 10%. Many students assume the two changes cancel out and the final answer is 0%. This is not correct, and it is important to understand why.

**Why simple addition does not work**

When you increase a number by 10%, the number becomes bigger.
When you then decrease this new (bigger) number by 10%, you are taking 10% of a bigger number, so the decrease is a bigger amount than the original increase.

This is why the two changes do not cancel out.

**Formula for combining two successive percentage changes**

If a quantity changes by a% and then by b% (one after the other), the total (net) percentage change is:

Net % change = a + b + (a × b)/100

Use a positive number for an increase and a negative number for a decrease.

**Example 5**
A number is increased by 10% and then decreased by 10%. Find the net percentage change.

Here, a = 10 (increase) and b = −10 (decrease)

Net change = 10 + (−10) + (10 × −10)/100
Net change = 0 + (−100/100)
Net change = 0 − 1
Net change = −1%

So the final value is actually 1% less than the original value, not the same.

**Checking with actual numbers**

Let us verify this with a number, say 100.

Step 1: Increase 100 by 10%.
100 × 1.10 = 110

Step 2: Decrease 110 by 10%.
110 × 0.90 = 99

Final value is 99, which is indeed 1% less than 100. This confirms our formula.

**Example 6**
The price of an article is increased by 25% and then decreased by 20%. Find the net change.

a = 25, b = −20

Net change = 25 + (−20) + (25 × −20)/100
Net change = 5 + (−500/100)
Net change = 5 − 5
Net change = 0%

So in this case, there is no change in the final price.

**Example 7 (Three successive changes)**

A number is increased by 10%, then increased by 20%, then decreased by 10%. Find the net percentage change.

Since there are three changes, we combine them two at a time.

Step 1: Combine the first two changes (10% and 20%).
Net = 10 + 20 + (10 × 20)/100 = 30 + 2 = 32%

Step 2: Now combine this result (32%) with the third change (−10%).
Net = 32 + (−10) + (32 × −10)/100 = 22 + (−3.2) = 18.8%

So the overall change is an increase of 18.8%.

## 7. Price and Consumption Problems

This is a common type of problem: if the price of something increases, by how much should you reduce how much you buy, so that your total spending stays the same?

**If price increases by x%, the reduction needed in consumption is:**

Required % decrease in consumption = [x / (100 + x)] × 100

**If price decreases by x%, the increase allowed in consumption is:**

Required % increase in consumption = [x / (100 − x)] × 100

**Example 8**
The price of sugar increases by 25%. By what percentage should a family reduce its sugar consumption so that their total spending on sugar does not change?

Here, x = 25 (price increase)

Required decrease = [25 / (100 + 25)] × 100
Required decrease = [25 / 125] × 100
Required decrease = 20%

So the family should buy 20% less sugar to spend the same amount of money.

**Why this works (in simple terms)**

Total spending = Price × Quantity

If price goes up, and we want total spending to stay the same, quantity must go down. The formula above tells us exactly how much quantity must go down, based on how much the price went up.

**Example 9**
The price of rice falls by 20%. By what percentage can a person increase their rice consumption, while spending the same amount as before?

Here, x = 20 (price decrease)

Required increase = [20 / (100 − 20)] × 100
Required increase = [20 / 80] × 100
Required increase = 25%

So the person can buy 25% more rice for the same money.

## 8. Simple Word Problems Based on Percentage

**Population Growth**

If a population P grows at r% every year, then after n years, the new population is:

New population = P × (1 + r/100)^n

**Example 10**
The population of a village is 20,000 and grows at 5% per year. Find the population after 2 years.

Step 1: Write the formula.
New population = 20000 × (1 + 5/100)^2
New population = 20000 × (1.05)^2

Step 2: Calculate (1.05)^2 = 1.1025

Step 3: Multiply.
New population = 20000 × 1.1025 = 22,050

**Pass/Fail Marks Problem**

**Example 11**
A student needs 40% marks to pass an exam. He scored 178 marks and failed by 22 marks. Find the maximum marks of the exam.

Step 1: Find the minimum passing marks.
If he failed by 22 marks, it means the passing marks are 22 more than what he scored.
Passing marks = 178 + 22 = 200

Step 2: We know that 40% of the maximum marks equals the passing marks.
40% of Maximum marks = 200

Step 3: Solve for maximum marks.
Maximum marks = 200 / (40/100)
Maximum marks = 200 / 0.40
Maximum marks = 500

**Election Problem**

**Example 12**
In an election between two candidates, the winner got 60% of the total votes and won by 4,800 votes. Find the total number of votes.

Step 1: If the winner got 60% of votes, the loser got the remaining 40%.

Step 2: The winning margin is the difference between their vote shares.
Margin % = 60% − 40% = 20%

Step 3: This 20% of the total votes equals the actual winning margin, 4,800 votes.
20% of Total votes = 4800

Step 4: Solve for total votes.
Total votes = 4800 / (20/100)
Total votes = 4800 / 0.20
Total votes = 24,000
